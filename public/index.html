Graph Fundamentals & Types
[TOPIC_01]

Graphs & Simple Graphs

A graph $G = (V, E)$ with no self-loops or parallel edges. The simplest foundation of graph theory — every vertex connects to others through unique, undirected edges.

[TOPIC_02]

Empty, Null & Trivial Graphs

Explore the boundary cases: empty graphs ($E = \emptyset$), null graphs ($V = \emptyset$), and trivial graphs consisting of a single isolated vertex with no connections.

[TOPIC_03]

Connected & Complete Graphs

In connected graphs, every vertex is reachable from any other. Complete graphs ($K_n$) take this further — every pair of distinct vertices has a direct edge between them.

[TOPIC_04]

Subgraphs & Spanning Subgraphs

A subgraph $H \subseteq G$ uses a subset of vertices and edges. Spanning subgraphs preserve all vertices of $G$ but may omit some edges.

[TOPIC_05]

Complement & Bipartite Graphs

The complement $\bar{G}$ inverts all edge connections. Bipartite graphs partition vertices into two disjoint sets $V_1$ and $V_2$, with edges only between the two sets.

[TOPIC_06]

Order, Size & Graph Classes

Order $|V|$ counts vertices, size $|E|$ counts edges. Classify graphs into paths ($P_n$), cycles ($C_n$), stars ($S_n$), and other structural families.

[TOPIC_07]

Regularity & Adjacencies

In $k$-regular graphs, every vertex has exactly degree $k$. Adjacent vertices share an edge; incident edges share a common vertex endpoint.

[TOPIC_08]

Degree Sequences

The non-increasing sequence of vertex degrees uniquely characterises a graph. The Handshaking Lemma: $\sum \deg(v) = 2|E|$ — the sum of degrees is always even.
